The platform

Everything a municipality needs to see, decide and act — in one place.

Real-time command map

Every incident, responder and evacuation center on one live map. Updates stream in over secure WebSockets the moment they happen in the field.

Priority engine

Incidents are scored automatically by hazard type, severity, people affected and time waiting, so the most urgent calls rise to the top.

Evacuation tracking

Barangays check families in and out of shelters. Capacity and overflow risk are visible to command instantly.

Targeted alerts

Broadcast advisories to everyone, a single barangay, or just responders — delivered live with a full delivery count.

Accountable by design

Role-based access for every user, and a tamper-evident audit trail of each sign-in, decision and status change.

Field-ready mobile apps

Citizens report emergencies with GPS and photos in seconds. Responders receive assignments, navigate, and update status from the scene.

SOS

How it works

From first report to full recovery, nothing falls through the cracks.

  1. 01

    Report

    A citizen taps SOS or a barangay official logs an incident. Location, photos and casualty counts are attached automatically.

  2. 02

    Verify

    The command center validates the report. The priority engine scores urgency, and barangays can escalate local emergencies.

  3. 03

    Dispatch

    Officers assign the nearest available responders and resources. Teams update their status live from the field.

  4. 04

    Recover

    Shelters are tracked, damage is assessed and relief requests are fulfilled — then every action is reported and archived.

Preparedness is not a single moment. It is a promise kept every day — by a whole community, working from the same picture.

Be prepared

What every household should know.

  • Pack a go-bag for 3 days: water, ready-to-eat food, medicines, flashlight, power bank, IDs and cash in a waterproof pouch.
  • Know your evacuation center and two safe routes to reach it from home, school and work.
  • Agree on a family plan — a meeting point and one out-of-area contact everyone can call.
  • Follow official channels for weather bulletins and DRRMO advisories; ignore unverified posts.
  • Evacuate early when advised. Do not wait for water to rise or winds to peak.
  • Avoid floodwater — just 15 cm of moving water can knock an adult down.
  • Switch off electricity and gas at the main if it is safe to do so before leaving.
  • Report emergencies with your exact location and the number of people who need help.
  • Return only when cleared by authorities that your area is safe.
  • Watch for hazards: downed power lines, weakened structures, gas leaks and contaminated water.
  • Document damage with photos and report it to your barangay for assessment and relief.
  • Check on neighbors — especially seniors, persons with disability and families with infants.

In an emergency

Call first. Every second matters.

If life is in danger, call the national emergency hotline immediately. Stay on the line and give your exact location.

  • 911 National Emergency HotlinePolice · Fire · Medical · Rescue
  • 143 Philippine Red CrossEmergency & disaster assistance
